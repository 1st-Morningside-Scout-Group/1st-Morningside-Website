Scouter's Review: Rudi Kruger

And just like that, we see the end of yet another weekend.

Kim Shield 30 years of putting Scouts through what I thought was a silly scouting event with a couple of tests and a little hike, but boy, was I wrong.

What I had seen those last 2 days, vomit, sweat, tears, blisters, mostly from other patrols, but I do have to admit, I did see some sweat from 1st Morningside patrols and some very, very tired scouts. I also got to see each and every one of those scouts getting up on Sunday morning 05:30 get dressed, pack their bags for the day and report to HQ for another long day in the heat. Did I mention the heat ? It was hot, very hot.

As a scouter and more so as a parent, I was proud to see what greatness our kids can achieve, the grit and strength, from down in the dumps to standing proudly on the parade ground heads high, representing 1st Morningside Scouts.

To each and every scout, well done, it was hard work but you did it. And, we don't even know it all...

You can be proud of yourselves.

Buffalo - Patrol 49

PL's Review: Oliver Kruger

Kim Shield went well overall. I feel that both patrols participated with cheerful spirits and put in a lot of effort throughout the event. There were a few minor mishaps, particularly with tents having broken or incorrect poles and the wrong dining shelter being brought. However, the Scouts were resourceful and managed to find solutions to these problems despite the setbacks.

Both patrols also had to adjust to early bedtimes and early wake-ups in order to have enough energy for the long hike. Although this was challenging, the Scouts managed to push through and participate in the activities.

I feel that the food was sufficient for the camp. While it was not anything particularly special, it provided what we needed, and I did not expect much more from the food at a camp like this.

Overall, I believe Kim Shield was a positive experience. Despite some equipment issues and the challenges of the schedule, both patrols showed good effort, maintained positive spirits, and were able to adapt when things did not go according to plan.

APL's Review: Liam Smith

Overall, I feel that Kim Shield went well, and I did not experience any major problems apart from the food. The food was not particularly enjoyable or filling enough, which resulted in some of the Scouts being reluctant to eat. This became a concern because the Scouts needed enough food and energy to participate fully in the activities, especially during the long hike.

For future events, I feel that more attention should be given to providing meals that are both nutritious and filling, while also being enjoyable enough to encourage the Scouts to eat properly. Ensuring that the Scouts are eating enough would help maintain their energy levels and prevent unnecessary fatigue during physically demanding activities.

Apart from this, I feel that the event went well overall and that the Scouts participated positively and put in good effort throughout the camp.

Scout's Review: Michaela Smith

Overall, I enjoyed the field day camp, especially the hiking and the opportunity to socialise with everyone. The activities were fun and it was nice spending time with friends. However, there were a few things that could be improved. The weather was very hot, and some people became dehydrated, so having more easily accessible drinking water and more opportunities to cool down would have helped. The food was also not the best and could be improved in terms of quality and variety. The camp bathrooms were quite dingy and could have been cleaner and better maintained. Overall, I had a good time, particularly during the hiking and social activities. With improvements to the food, bathrooms, and hydration arrangements, I think the camp could be an even better experience next time.

Replacement APL's (3rd) Review: Ndivhuwo Magidimisa

I believe that Kim Shield went relatively well, especially considering that both Patrol Leaders (PLs) had little to no previous experience leading a patrol, let alone leading one at a provincial Scouting event. Despite this, we were able to manage the patrols and complete the event reasonably successfully.

I feel that the area where we were lacking the most, and which ultimately cost us significantly, was our pre-event preparation. We did not conduct a proper equipment test and inspection before the event. We only realised how important this was once we arrived and discovered several equipment problems. These included tents having mismatched poles, not having all the correct dining tent poles, and having the incorrect size of dining shelter. This created unnecessary difficulties that could have been avoided through a proper equipment check beforehand.

Another area that we need to improve is managing the Scouts' energy levels during hikes and other physically demanding activities. We did not adequately ensure that everyone was getting enough electrolytes and carbohydrates throughout the hike, which resulted in several Scouts becoming ill. This showed us that hydration and nutrition need to be planned and monitored more carefully during future events.

Blesbok's first aid kit was also not adequately equipped to deal with some of the injuries and medical situations that occurred. We did not have sufficient supplies to properly assist with larger cuts to the hands, bee stings, blisters, or dehydration. This is something we need to address before our next major event to ensure that we are properly prepared to handle a wider range of first aid situations.

Overall, I believe that the camp went reasonably well, especially given the limited leadership experience within the patrols. However, there are several important lessons that we can take from this event. Better preparation, equipment inspections, proper hydration and nutrition planning, and a more comprehensive first aid kit would significantly improve our performance at future events.
